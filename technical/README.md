In one of my previous projects, I worked on a **document management system where document indexing and search** were critical parts of the application. The system was handling a large volume of documents and metadata, and one of the requirements was to **retrieve folder-size information** in near real time. The existing implementation used OpenSearch with synchronous APIs, but as the data volume and performance requirements increased, we needed a more scalable approach. I worked on designing and implementing asynchronous REST APIs using Elasticsearch and Kafka, where Kafka was used to decouple the processing flow and Elasticsearch handled document indexing, searching, and aggregation queries. The Elasticsearch-based microservices were designed to index and search more than 50 million records while maintaining sub-second search responses for business-critical document management workflows. The **new asynchronous approach also reduced the average API response time** by approximately 60% compared with the previous synchronous implementation, with the results integrated into React.js frontend components.

One of the more complex aspects of the work was the **migration from OpenSearch to Elasticsearch****. Rather than tightly coupling the application to Elasticsearch, I worked on making the indexing layer generic so that the same application flow could support multiple indexing technologies, including Solr 6, OpenSearch, and Elasticsearch**. The underlying implementation could select the appropriate indexing provider based on configuration, while keeping the business logic independent of the specific technology. I also worked with Elasticsearch aggregation queries for calculating and retrieving folder-level information efficiently. This required understanding the indexing strategy, query and aggregation behavior, asynchronous communication, service boundaries, and the impact of these decisions on performance. The experience gave me a deeper understanding of designing scalable and technology-independent components, rather than focusing only on implementing individual APIs. It also helped me develop a stronger architectural mindset around scalability, abstraction, migration, and long-term maintainability.
