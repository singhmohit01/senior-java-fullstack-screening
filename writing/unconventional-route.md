# Unconventional Route

One experience that comes to mind was in one of my previous projects, where we had an existing approach that was working well for the requirements at that time. As the project grew, however, the amount of data and the expectations around performance also increased. Instead of continuing with the same approach and making small improvements, I felt it was important to step back and look at the problem differently. I discussed the situation with the team and suggested trying a different approach that could handle the changing requirements more effectively, even though it meant moving away from something that was already working.

Taking a different route required me to spend time understanding the problem, comparing the available options, and making sure that the change would actually provide a meaningful benefit. It also meant being comfortable with some uncertainty because changing an existing approach always comes with its own risks. The experience taught me that taking an unconventional route does not mean choosing something different just for the sake of it. Sometimes it means having the confidence to question an existing approach, understand why it may no longer be the best fit, and take a calculated decision based on the needs of the project. It also taught me the importance of being open to change while making sure that the decision is supported by proper analysis and reasoning.
